Why the accent is indigo, and why the dark button label is black

The first draft's text-3 failed in both themes — 3.57 dark, 2.98 light against surface-2. It carries captions, the autosave stamp and result counts, which is real text. Revised to #868D9B / #656C7A.

Borders needed splitting. Decorative card borders can be low-contrast, but an input's border is the affordance telling you it's editable — that needs 3:1. border managed 1.31–1.40. Hence a separate border-input token.

A dark-theme filled button cannot have a white label. This is algebra, not taste: white-on-accent ≥ 4.5 requires accent luminance ≤ 0.183, while accent-on-dark-panel ≥ 4.5 requires ≥ 0.229. No colour satisfies both. So dark theme uses a light accent with a near-black label (on-accent: #0F1115), and light theme the reverse. Semi.Avalonia handles this correctly already — the point is that our overrides must not break it.

Indigo beat blue on the one thing that mattered. Teal and cyan fail as light fills (white-on-#0D9488 is 3.74). Every blue accessible enough to be a light-theme fill sits within 1.19 contrast of the document default #2563EB — visually the same colour. Indigo passes everything and sits 1.88 away, the largest separation among accessible candidates.

The rule this all serves: UI accent is not document accent

Resume.AccentColor is user data; it drives the rendered PDF and defaults to #2563EB. ui-accent is fixed chrome. They must never be bound to one another — otherwise picking a magenta résumé restyles every focus ring, selection highlight and primary button in the app.

Document accent may appear in exactly two places: inside the preview page, and in the swatch/hex control that edits it. Nowhere else.
